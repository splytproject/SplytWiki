**Satoshi Nakamoto** is the name used by the unknown person or group of people who created [Bitcoin](../Bitcoin/), wrote its white paper and developed its original software.[^whitepaper] Nakamoto was active publicly from 2008 to 2011, communicating only by email and on internet forums, and then stopped all contact. Despite many investigations and several people claiming the title, Nakamoto's real identity has never been established.

Nakamoto is believed to control a large number of bitcoins mined in the network's first year, which have never been moved. The smallest unit of bitcoin, one hundred-millionth of a coin, is called a **satoshi** in Nakamoto's honour.

## Development of Bitcoin

Nakamoto registered the domain *bitcoin.org* in August 2008 and on 31 October 2008 announced the Bitcoin white paper on a cryptography mailing list, writing: "I've been working on a new electronic cash system that's fully peer-to-peer, with no trusted third party."[^mailing-list] The paper cited earlier ideas including Adam Back's *Hashcash* and Wei Dai's *b-money*.

On 3 January 2009 Nakamoto mined the first block of the Bitcoin [blockchain](../Blockchain/), the *genesis block*, and on 9 January released the first version of the Bitcoin software. Nakamoto continued as the project's lead developer through 2009 and 2010, fixing bugs, adding features and discussing the design with early users on the Bitcoin forum, which Nakamoto set up.

## Disappearance

In mid-2010 Nakamoto began handing control of the project to the developer Gavin Andresen, along with other contributors. Nakamoto's last post on the Bitcoin forum was made in December 2010. In an email sent in April 2011, Nakamoto wrote of having "moved on to other things", and there has been no verified communication since.

## Characteristics

Nakamoto's online profile on the P2P Foundation website claimed to be a 37-year-old man living in Japan.[^p2p] However, Nakamoto wrote fluent English, often using British spellings such as "colour" and "optimise", and the timing of posts did not match typical waking hours in Japan, leading many to doubt the claim. Some researchers have suggested that "Satoshi Nakamoto" may have been a group rather than one individual.

### Bitcoin holdings

By analysing patterns in the earliest blocks, researchers have estimated that a single miner, widely assumed to be Nakamoto, mined around one million bitcoins in 2009 and 2010. These coins have never been spent. Because the holdings would be worth a very large sum, any movement of them would be closely watched as a possible sign of Nakamoto's return or identity.

## Identity

Many people have been suggested as Nakamoto, and several have denied it. Notable cases include:

- **Hal Finney**, a cryptographer who received the first Bitcoin transaction from Nakamoto. He denied being Nakamoto and died in 2014.
- **Nick Szabo**, a computer scientist who designed a precursor system called *bit gold* and coined the term [smart contract](../Smart_contract/). He has denied being Nakamoto.
- **Dorian Nakamoto**, a Japanese-American engineer named as the creator by *Newsweek* in March 2014.[^newsweek] He denied any involvement, and the claim was widely rejected.
- **Craig Wright**, an Australian computer scientist who publicly claimed from 2016 onwards to be Nakamoto and brought lawsuits on that basis. In March 2024 the High Court of England and Wales ruled that he is not Satoshi Nakamoto and did not write the Bitcoin white paper; the judge later found that he had forged documents on a large scale to support his claim.[^copa]
- **Peter Todd**, a Bitcoin developer named in the 2024 HBO documentary *Money Electric: The Bitcoin Mystery*. He denied being Nakamoto.

## Legacy

Nakamoto is widely regarded as one of the most influential and mysterious figures in the history of technology. Bitcoin's success inspired thousands of other [cryptocurrencies](../Cryptocurrency/) and the wider [blockchain](../Blockchain/) industry. Nakamoto's anonymity is also seen by many supporters as important to Bitcoin itself: with no known founder, there is no leader who can be pressured, arrested or relied upon. A bronze bust of Nakamoto with a reflective face, intended to show that "we are all Satoshi", was unveiled in Budapest in 2021.
