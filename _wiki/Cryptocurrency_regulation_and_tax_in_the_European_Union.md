> **In plain terms:** The EU has one rulebook for crypto, called MiCA. A company licensed in one EU country can serve customers in all of them, and stablecoin issuers must hold reserves and publish information. Tax is different: each country sets its own crypto tax rules, though since 2026 platforms must report users' transactions to tax authorities.

The **Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation** (MiCA), Regulation (EU) 2023/1114, is the European Union's framework for crypto-assets that are not already covered by financial-services law.[^mica] It regulates issuers of tokens and stablecoins, and **crypto-asset service providers** (CASPs) such as exchanges and custodians.[^mica] It applied in full from 30 December 2024, and its transition period for existing firms ended on 1 July 2026.[^mica][^esma-mica]

MiCA does not cover tax. Each member state taxes crypto under its own law. EU directives require platforms to report users' transactions to tax authorities from 2026.[^dac8]

## What MiCA covers

MiCA sorts crypto-assets into three groups:[^mica]

| Type | What it is | Main rules |
|---|---|---|
| **E-money token** (EMT) | A token that keeps a stable value by referring to one official currency | Issuer must be a bank or e-money institution; holders can redeem at par (Title IV) |
| **Asset-referenced token** (ART) | A token that refers to another value, right or basket | Issuer authorisation, reserve assets, own funds (Title III) |
| **Other crypto-assets** | Everything else in scope, such as most utility tokens | A white paper must be published and notified (Title II) |

Out of scope are crypto-assets that are already financial instruments, such as tokenised shares, and unique, non-fungible tokens.[^mica] Crypto-assets with no identifiable issuer, such as bitcoin, are outside the issuer rules. Firms providing services in them still need CASP authorisation.[^mica]

### Stablecoin limits

Large ARTs and EMTs face stricter rules and supervision by the European Banking Authority.[^mica] Where a stablecoin linked to a non-euro currency is widely used as a means of exchange, issuance can be capped. The threshold is about one million transactions or €200 million a day within the EU.[^mica]

### Crypto-asset service providers

Firms that run trading platforms, exchange crypto, provide custody, execute orders or give advice must be authorised as CASPs by a national authority.[^mica] Authorisation in one member state lets a firm "passport" its services to all others.[^mica] CASPs must meet capital, governance, custody and conflict-of-interest rules. Customer assets must be kept separate from the firm's own.[^mica]

As of October 2026, ESMA's register listed several hundred authorised CASPs.[^esma-mica][^outrun] Firms serving EU clients without authorisation after 1 July 2026 must stop.[^esma-mica]

### Market abuse

MiCA bans insider dealing, unlawful disclosure of inside information and market manipulation in crypto-assets. Issuers must disclose inside information.[^mica]

## Anti-money laundering

### Travel rule

The **Transfer of Funds Regulation** (EU) 2023/1113 applies the "travel rule" to crypto. CASPs must send and receive information on the originator and beneficiary of every crypto transfer, with no minimum amount.[^tfr] For transfers over €1,000 to or from a **self-hosted wallet**, a CASP must take steps to check that its customer owns or controls that wallet.[^tfr]

### Anonymous accounts

The **Anti-Money Laundering Regulation** (EU) 2024/1624 bars banks, financial institutions and CASPs from keeping anonymous accounts. That includes crypto-asset accounts that allow anonymisation, such as those for privacy-enhancing coins. The ban applies from 10 July 2027.[^amlr]

## Taxation

### National tax rules

There is no EU crypto tax. Rates, holding-period rules and treatment of staking differ by country. For example, Germany treats private sales of crypto as private disposals under § 23 of its Income Tax Act (EStG).[^bmf-2025] Gains on crypto held for more than one year are generally tax-free there. The Federal Ministry of Finance set out detailed guidance in a letter of 6 March 2025.[^bmf-2025]

### DAC8 reporting

**DAC8**, Directive (EU) 2023/2226, brings the OECD's Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework into EU law.[^dac8] From 1 January 2026, CASPs must collect and report their users' identities and transactions to the tax authority of their home state. Authorities exchange this data with other member states.[^dac8]

## Supervision: the ESMA proposal

Today national authorities authorise and supervise CASPs, with the European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA) co-ordinating. On 4 December 2025 the European Commission proposed a "market integration and supervision" package.[^dla-package] Under it, ESMA would directly supervise all CASPs.[^dla-package][^stobox] ESMA welcomed the proposal.[^esma-proposal]

As of October 2026 the proposal is under negotiation. Many member states favour ESMA supervising only significant CASPs, leaving the rest with national authorities.[^stobox] Negotiations were expected to run until at least 2027.[^dla-package]

## Criticism and open debates

- **Who supervises.** The Commission's text would give ESMA every CASP, while most member states prefer ESMA to take only significant firms.[^dla-package][^stobox]
- **Stablecoin limits.** The caps on non-euro stablecoins aim to protect monetary sovereignty, but limit the use of dollar stablecoins in the EU.[^mica]
- **Self-hosted wallets and privacy.** The travel rule's checks on self-hosted wallets and the 2027 ban on anonymous accounts are criticised as reducing financial privacy. Lawmakers justify them as anti-money-laundering measures.[^tfr][^amlr]
- **Gaps.** MiCA leaves most decentralised finance, lending and NFTs outside its scope; the Commission must report on them.[^mica]

## Practical notes

- Check a provider on ESMA's interim MiCA register before using it.[^esma-mica]
- Expect platforms to ask for tax residence details under DAC8, and for information about your own wallets under the travel rule.[^dac8][^tfr]
- Tax depends on your country of residence; see your national tax authority.
- This article is general information, not tax or legal advice.
