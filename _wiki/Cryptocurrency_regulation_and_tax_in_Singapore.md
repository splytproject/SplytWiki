> **In plain terms:** Singapore lets crypto businesses operate, but only with a licence from its central bank, the Monetary Authority of Singapore. Licensed firms must protect customers' coins and may not lure ordinary investors with bonuses or credit. Singapore has no capital gains tax, so long-term investors usually pay no tax on profits. People who trade crypto as a business are taxed on their gains.

Singapore regulates crypto through the **Monetary Authority of Singapore** (MAS), which is both central bank and financial regulator. Most cryptocurrencies are **digital payment tokens** (DPTs) under the **Payment Services Act 2019** (PS Act). Firms dealing in or exchanging them must be licensed.[^mas-psact] Since 30 June 2025 Singapore companies serving only overseas customers also need a licence, which MAS grants only in "extremely limited" cases.[^ag-fsma]

For tax, Singapore does not tax capital gains. Gains from trading tokens as a business are taxable income.[^iras-income] Supplies of payment tokens have been exempt from goods and services tax (GST) since 1 January 2020.[^iras-gst]

## Regulation

### Licensing under the Payment Services Act

The PS Act requires a licence for **DPT services**, such as buying and selling tokens for customers or running an exchange.[^mas-psact] Its scope widened on 4 April 2024 to cover custody and transfers of DPTs.[^mas-psact] Licensed firms must follow MAS Notice PSN02 on anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing.[^mas-psact]

### Consumer protection

Between 2022 and 2024 MAS tightened rules for retail customers. DPT service providers must:[^mas-2023-consumer][^mas-2023-custody][^mas-psg03]

- hold customers' tokens on a **statutory trust**, separate from the firm's own assets;
- not facilitate **lending or staking** of retail customers' tokens;
- test a new retail customer's **risk awareness** before they trade;
- not offer **incentives** to trade, such as free tokens or trading credits;
- not provide **financing, margin or leverage** to retail customers;
- not accept payment by **locally issued credit or charge cards**;
- limit how much crypto counts towards a customer's net worth when assessing them.

MAS warns that these measures "cannot insulate customers from losses" from the "inherently speculative and highly risky nature" of cryptocurrency trading.[^mas-2023-consumer]

### Firms serving only overseas customers

Part 9 of the **Financial Services and Markets Act 2022** (FSMA) took effect on 30 June 2025. It requires a licence for Singapore firms, or individuals with a Singapore base, that provide digital token services only outside Singapore.[^ag-fsma] There was no transition period; unlicensed firms had to stop by that date.[^ag-fsma] MAS said it would grant such licences "only in extremely limited circumstances", citing money-laundering risks.[^ag-fsma]

### Stablecoins

MAS finalised a framework for **single-currency stablecoins** pegged to the Singapore dollar or a G10 currency in August 2023.[^mas-scs-2023] Issuers must meet rules on reserve assets and hold minimum capital. They must "return the par value of SCS to holders within five business days" of a request.[^mas-scs-2023] Only stablecoins meeting the rules may be called "MAS-regulated stablecoins".[^mas-scs-2023]

On 1 September 2026 MAS consulted on draft amendments to the PS Act to put the framework into law. They would create a stablecoin-issuance licence and let MAS recognise some foreign stablecoins.[^mas-scs-2026] Stablecoins outside the framework are treated as ordinary DPTs.[^mas-scs-2026]

### Enforcement: Three Arrows Capital

Three Arrows Capital was a Singapore-based crypto hedge fund that collapsed in 2022. In June 2022 MAS reprimanded it for giving false information and exceeding its fund-manager limits.[^mas-3ac-2022] On 13 September 2023 MAS issued nine-year prohibition orders against founders Zhu Su and Kyle Davies. MAS cited their failure to maintain risk management for the firm's crypto investments.[^mas-3ac-2023]

## Taxation

### Income tax

The Inland Revenue Authority of Singapore (IRAS) applies normal income tax rules to digital tokens.[^iras-income]

- **Capital gains are not taxed.** An individual who buys tokens as a long-term investment usually pays no tax on the gain.
- **Trading gains are taxed.** Gains are taxable if the activity amounts to trading. IRAS judges this by the "badges of trade", such as how often and how long tokens are held.[^iras-income]
- **Tokens received as payment** for goods, services or work are taxable income.[^iras-income]

### GST

From 1 January 2020, using payment tokens to pay for goods or services is no longer a taxable supply of the tokens. Exchanging them for fiat or other payment tokens is an exempt supply.[^iras-gst] The change applies to payment tokens, not to utility or security tokens.[^iras-gst]

## Criticism and open debates

- **Overseas-only firms.** With licences for overseas-only services granted only in "extremely limited" cases, such firms had to leave or stop by June 2025.[^ag-fsma]
- **Retail restrictions.** The ban on incentives and credit cards limits marketing, but MAS says it cannot prevent losses.[^mas-2023-consumer]
- **Stablecoin delay.** The 2023 framework was not yet law in October 2026; industry had expected legislation by late 2025.[^mas-scs-2026]
- **Fund oversight.** MAS found Three Arrows Capital's directors had failed to maintain risk management for its crypto investments.[^mas-3ac-2023]

## Practical notes

- Check that a platform is licensed by MAS before using it.[^mas-psact]
- Expect a risk-awareness test before trading on a licensed platform.[^mas-psg03]
- Whether gains are taxable depends on the facts; IRAS assesses them case by case.[^iras-income]
- This article is general information, not tax or legal advice.
