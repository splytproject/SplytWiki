This article summarizes the legal and economic landscape for cryptocurrencies in the Russian Federation as of 2026. It covers legal status, regulation and licensing, taxation, mining, exchanges and recent events. Statements are supported by official regulator releases, government sources and reputable reporting — see References.

## Summary
- Cryptocurrencies are legal in Russia but are not legal tender and cannot be used as a national means of payment inside the country. They are treated as property/digital assets and regulated accordingly.[^cbr]
- In 2026 the State adopted a comprehensive crypto law establishing licensing for exchanges and service providers, investor categories, and a transition timetable for compliance.[^mt2026][^cbr]
- Mining and related activities are permitted; professional operators must register and comply with tax and reporting rules. Proposals to criminalize large-scale unregistered mining have been discussed but implementation varies by region and remains subject to federal legislation.[^coinpedia]
- Tax authorities require declaration of crypto holdings and taxable events; income from trading, mining or sale of crypto is taxable under the tax code and guidance published by the Ministry of Finance and tax authorities.[^taxatlas]

## Legal status and definitions
Russian federal legislation classifies crypto and similar instruments as property or "digital financial assets" rather than money or legal tender. This classification means:

- Crypto cannot be used to pay for goods and services domestically; the Russian ruble remains the only legal tender.[^cbr]
- Digital assets, tokens and rights can be issued, transferred and held subject to rules on registration, disclosure and licensing introduced by the 2026 regulatory framework.[^mt2026]

The 2026 law sets out categories of market participants (issuers, operators, custodians, exchanges) and gives the Central Bank and Ministry of Finance powers to regulate licensing, investor protections, and AML/KYC rules.[^cbr][^gate]

## Regulation, licensing and investor protections
Key regulatory features introduced or clarified in 2026 include:

- Licensing: Crypto exchanges, brokers, custodians and other service providers operating in Russia must obtain a license from the Central Bank (transition deadlines apply, with phased compliance through mid-2027).[^
cbr][^mt2026]
- Investor categories: The law distinguishes qualified and non-qualified investors; non-qualified investors face limits on purchase volumes and educational/knowledge checks to access certain products.[^gate]
- AML / KYC: Russia’s anti-money-laundering framework now explicitly covers crypto intermediaries; banks and payment institutions must monitor and may be required to block or report suspicious transfers to/from unauthorised crypto service providers.[^mt2026][^gate]

These measures are intended to reduce systemic risk and align exchange operations with banking-sector oversight.[^cbr]

## Taxation and reporting
The Russian tax authorities treat cryptocurrencies as taxable property. Practical implications:

- Individuals and businesses must declare crypto holdings and taxable events (sales, trading gains, mining income) on tax returns. Tax treatment depends on whether the activity is occasional (capital gains style) or carried out as business activity (ordinary income / corporate tax).[^
 taxatlas]
- Mining proceeds are taxable as income; professional miners operating at scale are expected to register and pay corporate or personal income tax as applicable.[^coinpedia]
- Cross-border holdings and foreign exchange into/from crypto must be reported; Russian taxpayers are subject to rules that require disclosure of foreign assets in some circumstances.[^cbr]

Tax administration guidance remains an active area of policy work; the Ministry of Finance has published explanatory notes and is drafting clarifications for miners and exchanges.[^coinpedia]

## Mining
Russia has been a substantial location for PoW mining due to available electricity and cold climate in some regions. Important points:

- Mining is permitted. Individuals mining for personal, non-commercial use generally fall outside registration requirements, while organised commercial mining operations must register and comply with business and environmental regulations.[^coinpedia]
- Several regions offer favourable electricity tariffs and have attracted professional mining farms; regulators monitor grid impacts and energy usage.[^coinpedia]
- There have been proposals to introduce penalties or criminal sanctions for large-scale unregistered mining, particularly where it strains local grids or is connected to illicit activity; these measures have been under discussion but require further legislative steps and regional enforcement.[^coinpedia]

## Exchanges and market infrastructure
- Exchanges operating in or targeting Russian customers are required to obtain a Central Bank license and implement AML/KYC, custody and reporting safeguards.[^cbr][^gate]
- International sanctions and banking restrictions have affected how foreign exchanges interact with Russian customers; peer-to-peer (P2P) markets remain an important on/off ramp but are increasingly subject to monitoring.[^mt2026]
- The law permits cross-border settlements in crypto under specified conditions; the government has signalled interest in using digital assets for some international trade and settlement use cases.[^mt2026]

## Payments, cross-border use and sanctions considerations
The 2026 framework preserves the ruble as the only legal domestic payment currency and retains restrictions on using crypto for ordinary domestic payments.[^cbr]
However, the law and related regulations allow for crypto and digital assets to be used in cross-border transactions under regulated conditions. Policymakers have framed this flexibility as a tool to facilitate international trade and to mitigate the effects of banking-sector sanctions.[^mt2026]

## AML, KYC and enforcement
Regulators require licensed crypto intermediaries to implement AML/KYC measures comparable to those in the banking sector. Reporting obligations and cooperation with law enforcement have been emphasised to combat money laundering, sanctions evasion, and other illegal uses of crypto.[^gate][^mt2026]

## Notable incidents and events (2024–2026)
- 2026: The State Duma passed a comprehensive crypto law and the President signed it into force; the Central Bank published implementation guidance and started a licensing timetable for exchanges and custodians.[^cbr][^mt2026]
- Ongoing: Enforcement actions against individual operators for non-compliance, plus investigations into hacks and illicit schemes, have been reported by industry press and major outlets; details and outcomes vary by case and are documented in news reports.[^coinpedia][^mt2026]

## Outlook and policy debates
- Regulators continue to fine-tune investor protections, tax rules for miners and companies, and standards for custody and market infrastructure.[^coinpedia]
- There is public and industry debate about the balance between using crypto to support cross-border trade and preventing illicit finance or sanctions evasion. The Central Bank emphasises stability; the Ministry of Finance and other agencies emphasise practical market access and tax collection.[^cbr][^coinpedia]
